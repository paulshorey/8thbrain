# Worker Impact and Power Dynamics in AI Knowledge Management

## Overview

AI-powered knowledge management is not a neutral technology. It redistributes power between organizations and workers, extracts and commodifies tacit knowledge, and creates new forms of surveillance and control. The distributional consequences are systematically skewed. **[HIGH CONFIDENCE]**

## Who Benefits

### Organizations and executives

- McKinsey estimates AI could create up to **$4.4 trillion in global value**, with knowledge management as a primary vector
- Executives gain real-time access to organizational knowledge previously locked in employees' heads
- Organizations reduce dependency on individual knowledge holders ("key person risk")
- Knowledge becomes a corporate asset extractable from workers who generated it

### Technology vendors

- Enterprise AI-KM represents a massive market opportunity
- Vendors benefit regardless of whether deployments succeed (licenses, consulting, integration services)
- The $644B spent on GenAI in 2025 flowed primarily to technology companies

### Users with strong existing expertise

- Workers who already possess deep domain knowledge can use AI as a genuine force multiplier
- AI handles routine retrieval, freeing experts for higher-value synthesis
- But this benefit is conditional on maintaining expertise independently of AI

## Who Loses

### Knowledge workers broadly

McKinsey identifies knowledge workers as the **"biggest losers of the AI boom"** ([Bloomberg Law, 2024](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/daily-labor-report/biggest-losers-of-ai-boom-are-knowledge-workers-mckinsey-says)):

- Whole swaths of business activities (sales, marketing, customer operations) become embedded in software
- An estimated **300 million knowledge workers** could face displacement
- Workers lose bargaining power as their knowledge becomes system-encoded
- Entry-level knowledge workers are disproportionately affected as AI handles tasks that previously served as training grounds

### Workers whose knowledge is extracted

AI-KM systems mine vast volumes of work-related data to extract and surface knowledge ([Microsoft Research, 2023](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/a-framework-for-exploring-the-consequences-of-ai-mediated-enterprise-knowledge-access-and-identifying-risks-to-workers/)). Specific risks:

- **Commodification:** Worker knowledge and expertise is converted into a corporate asset without proportionate compensation
- **Appropriation:** Intellectual contributions are captured by the system and attributed to the organization
- **Concentration of power:** Knowledge that was once distributed among workers becomes centralized in systems controlled by management
- **Marginalization:** Certain workers or roles are rendered less visible or valuable

### Workers designated as "experts"

AI systems automatically identify organizational "experts" based on content analysis ([Ethical and Social Considerations - arXiv, 2022](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2209.03819v1.pdf)):

- Identified experts may face increased, unsolicited workloads
- Inaccuracies in expert identification can lead to disproportionate harms
- Expert designation changes how individuals perceive themselves and how colleagues perceive them
- Workers have limited control over how their contributions are classified and surfaced

### Workers with less training

AI-KM systems disproportionately harm workers with less formal training or education:

- These workers' tacit knowledge is most easily replaced by AI
- They have fewer alternative career options
- The deskilling effect hits hardest at lower skill levels
- Wages are driven down as AI commodifies routine knowledge work

## The Corporate Surveillance Dimension

### AI-KM as a "looking glass"

AI knowledge systems function as mechanisms that both reflect and distort workers' contributions ([Through the Looking-Glass - arXiv, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.09410v2)):

- The visibility of certain work changes what gets acknowledged and valued
- Knowledge that the system captures becomes valued; knowledge it misses becomes invisible
- Workers may self-censor or modify behavior knowing their contributions are being monitored and extracted
- Three transparency gaps exist:
  - **System transparency:** How the system works (often opaque)
  - **Procedural transparency:** How decisions are made using system outputs (often undisclosed)
  - **Outcome transparency:** Effects on workers (often unmeasured)

### Power dynamics and resistance

Risks arise from interactions between technical systems and organizational power structures:

- Workers have begun organizing collective resistance to AI solutionism ([Resisting AI Solutionism - arXiv, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08313v1))
- Techno-deterministic assumptions about inevitable AI adoption are being questioned
- Unions and worker organizations are negotiating for protections against knowledge extraction
- But power asymmetries remain: organizations control the systems, the data, and the deployment decisions

## The Deskilling Feedback Loop

AI-KM creates a self-reinforcing cycle of skill degradation:

1. AI handles knowledge tasks that previously developed worker expertise
2. Workers' skills atrophy from disuse
3. Workers become more dependent on AI for tasks they could previously do independently
4. Organizations use increased AI dependency as justification for further automation
5. The workforce progressively loses the capacity to function without AI assistance

This is not hypothetical. Studies of endoscopists who routinely use AI assistance show measurable **performance deterioration** when AI is unavailable ([ACM Communications, 2025](https://cacm.acm.org/news/the-ai-deskilling-paradox/)).

## Strongest criticism

> AI knowledge management is, at its core, a mechanism for extracting knowledge from workers and encoding it as corporate property. The framing as "productivity enhancement" obscures the fundamental power transfer: workers generate knowledge through years of experience and tacit learning, AI systems capture and systematize that knowledge, and organizations gain permanent access while workers become replaceable. The $4.4 trillion in estimated value creation is not being created -- it is being transferred from labor to capital.
