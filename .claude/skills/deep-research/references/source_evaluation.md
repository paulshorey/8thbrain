# Source Evaluation Criteria

## Tier A - Primary Sources

A source qualifies as Tier A if it meets **all** of the following:

- Presents original data, research, or analysis (not summarizing others)
- Published by a recognized institution, peer-reviewed journal, or official body
- Has a clear publication date
- Author credentials are verifiable
- Methodology is described or referenced

**Examples**: Nature, Science, IEEE, ACM, government statistical agencies, RFC documents, original patent filings, court rulings, official corporate filings (SEC, annual reports).

## Tier B - Authoritative Secondary Sources

A source qualifies as Tier B if it meets **most** of the following:

- Written by a credentialed expert or established journalist
- Published on a reputable platform with editorial oversight
- References or cites primary sources
- Provides analysis, synthesis, or expert interpretation
- Has a clear publication date

**Examples**: The Economist, MIT Technology Review, Ars Technica, IEEE Spectrum, company engineering blogs (e.g., Google AI Blog, Meta Research), established think tank reports (Brookings, RAND), well-sourced Wikipedia articles.

## Tier C - Supplementary Sources

A source is Tier C if it provides useful context but lacks the rigor of Tiers A or B:

- Community-generated content with visible peer review (upvotes, comments, corrections)
- Tutorial or educational content from known platforms
- News reporting without deep analysis
- Personal blogs from practitioners with demonstrated expertise

**Examples**: Stack Overflow highly-voted answers, Hacker News discussions, educational YouTube channels, Medium posts by verified practitioners, Reddit AMAs with experts.

## Tier D - Discard

Discard a source if **any** of the following apply:

- No identifiable author or publication date
- Content is primarily AI-generated filler with no original insight
- The site is a known content farm or SEO-optimized aggregator
- Claims are made without any citations or evidence
- The information is demonstrably outdated and has been superseded
- The site has a clear commercial bias without disclosure

## Conflict Resolution

When sources disagree:

1. **Prefer higher-tier sources** (A over B, B over C)
2. **Prefer more recent sources** when facts may have changed
3. **Prefer primary data** over interpretations of that data
4. **Note the disagreement explicitly** in your research documentation
5. **Present both positions** when the disagreement is substantive and unresolved

## Citation Format

When recording sources, always capture:

- **Title** of the article or document
- **Author(s)** if available
- **Publication** or platform name
- **Date** of publication
- **URL** (full, not shortened)
- **Quality tier** (A/B/C)
- **Key claims** extracted from this source
